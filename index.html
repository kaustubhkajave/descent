01Epipelagic · 0 – 200 m

The Sunlight zone

Light pours in from above and fuels nearly all photosynthesis in the sea. The microscopic phytoplankton drifting here help make the air you're breathing right now.

~50%of Earth's oxygen is produced by ocean phytoplankton

02Mesopelagic · 200 – 1,000 m

The Twilight zone

Sunlight thins to a faint blue, too weak for plants. Every night, enormous numbers of animals rise from here toward the surface to feed, then sink back before dawn: the biggest daily migration on the planet.

<1%of surface sunlight reaches the top of this zone

03Bathypelagic · 1,000 – 4,000 m

The Midnight zone

No sunlight ever reaches this deep. The only light is made by the animals themselves, in flashes and glowing lures used to hunt, hide and find a mate.

Move your cursor through the dark

~4°Ctypical water temperature, all year round

04Abyssopelagic · 4,000 – 6,000 m

The Abyss

Near-freezing water over vast plains of fine sediment. Food arrives mostly as "marine snow": flakes of dead plankton and debris that take weeks to drift down from above.

~500×the air pressure at the surface, at 5,000 m

05Hadalpelagic · 6,000 – 10,935 m

The Hadal zone

Named after Hades, the Greek underworld. These are the trenches, deep scars where one tectonic plate dives beneath another. Your torch is the only light down here.

~1,100atmospheres of pressure at the bottom
